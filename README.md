# Optimal-Planting-Session-Prediction-by-using-ML-approach
## Abstract
Agricultural production in Bangladesh is extremely sensitive to climate variability. Climate change poses one of the greatest challenges to determining the optimal planting seasons in the country. With increasing climatic uncertainties, evidence-based planting decisions are essential to sustain crop yields and ensure food security. Climate change presents one of the greatest challenges to identifying optimal planting seasons, especially as historical calendars may no longer align with shifting environmental conditions. This study aims to address this gap to predict the optimal transplanting months for 103 major crops in Bangladesh using machine learning models and historical weather patterns. 
 
We employed the day-to-day meteorological information, like as temperature, rainfall, humidity, and sunshine, from 35 meteorological stations spanning the period 1961 to 2023 and crop-wise agronomic information for nine crop groups, like cereals, pulses, oil seeds, vegetables, fiber, staples, fruits, tubers, and nicotine. For modelling, we prepared four different datasets based on temporal span, variable combinations, and geographic coverage. On preprocessing, categorical variables such as crop names and types were numerically encoded, and continuous variables were scaled to standardize the input data. We subsequently employed different machine learning models, Decision Tree, K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN), and Random Forest to forecast optimum planting seasons. By using 10-fold cross-validation (10K-CV) in training and testing, the models performed highly well in classification, with Random Forest obtaining a 98.95% accuracy with a 30-year meteorological window of 1993–2023 with crop features, and geographical features, and KNN obtaining a 97.68% accuracy.

These results demonstrate the potential of machine learning to robustly and accurately identify suitable planting seasons for Bangladeshi crops. The outcomes favor the development of intelligent agro-advisory systems and climate-resilient farming calendars. Future work directions include integrating crop yield outcomes to forecast productivity as well as transplanting time and adapting the models for early-season, near-real-time weather input-based forecasting.
